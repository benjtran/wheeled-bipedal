Wheel coupling: counterbored adapter — why it slips & the fix

Current — slips

Adapter has round OD in a round counterbore

The hex keys the adapter to the coupler. But the adapter's round OD only sits in the wheel's round counterbore — that hop is friction-only.

  • Hex bore keys adapter → coupler ✓ (that's why both adapters stay locked to the motor).
  • Adapter OD → wheel counterbore is round-on-round = friction only ✗ — the wheel spins around the adapter.
  • Front disc is just a screw seat; the screw clamps axially, which can't stop rotation.
Proposed fix — positive drive

Make the adapter OD + counterbore hex

The adapter's outer shape and the wheel's counterbore are now both hex — torque flows coupler → adapter → wheel entirely through flats.

  • Reprint the adapter with a hex (or double-D) outer profile; cut the wheel's motor-side counterbore to match.
  • Now it's nested hexes: coupler → adapter → wheel — every hop keyed.
  • Screw returns to axial retention only.
  • Print: 4+ perimeters, 60–100% infill, layer lines perpendicular to the axle so the flats don't shear along a layer.
  • Add ~0.2 mm flat-to-flat clearance so it presses in without cracking the wheel.
No-reprint retrofits (do both wheels, powered down):
  • Anti-rotation pins: drill 2–3 holes straddling the adapter/counterbore seam and drop in short pins or filament — cheap and strong in shear.
  • Radial set screw: tap a hole through the wheel rim into the adapter OD (onto a filed flat).
  • Epoxy the adapter into its counterbore if it never needs to separate (roughen both faces first).
The hex counterbore is the permanent fix; retrofits get you rolling tonight.
